An agent with a wallet can pay anyone, any amount. Today the limit lives in a server that decides and then
signs, so the limit is a promise, not a constraint.

So I put the budget where the money is: a vault whose authority is a 2-of-2 SPL Token multisig, the agent's key
and a guard key. In the demo the agent builds its own payment and signs alone, and Solana refuses it, because the
vault is 2-of-2 and the agent is only half of it.

What: an on-chain spend policy for AI agents. Daily limit, per-transaction limit, recipient allowlist, kill
switch, and every decision recorded in a Memo inside the transaction that moves the money.

Why it matters: agents are getting wallets before they get limits, and "the service said yes" does not survive a
compromised agent or a bug in that service. Moving the budget into the vault changes the failure mode.

Why me: this MVP exists as of today. 12 passing policy tests, a live console on Devnet, a public repo. I ran it
against real Solana, it surfaced two real bugs, and I fixed them and added a typecheck to the build.
